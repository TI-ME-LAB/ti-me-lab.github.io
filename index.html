Translation-Informed Microbial Ecology

Exploring the microbial world from a translational perspective.
Bio

Kankan Zhao | 赵侃侃

My research centers on Translation-Informed Microbial Ecology (TIME). Genomes define what microbes are capable of; translation reveals what they are actually doing. To capture this activity, I apply metaRiboSeq, a community-scale ribosome profiling approach that measures active translation within microbial communities. Using this approach, I investigate how microbes allocate finite ribosomal resources. Across soil, marine, and host-associated ecosystems, communities partition translational investment rather than averaging it, and niche divergence, rather than richness, governs this structure, a pattern I term Translational Resource Partitioning (TRP). The same framework extends to viruses, including the share of translational capacity allocated to prophages. Building on these findings, I aim to harness microbial competition to design targeted interventions for soil health and pathogen displacement.

Outside the lab, I'm a longtime fan of progressive rock, post-rock, punk, post-punk, and electronic music — a taste that also feeds into my science communication work, where I convert biological data into electronic music.
